# Structure models (lenses)

Percentages are positions in the book by word count (cumulative % from the pacing map). They are
typical ranges, not targets.

## 1. Three-act structure

| Beat | Typical position | Function | Missing-beat symptom |
|---|---|---|---|
| hook / opening image | 0-2% | voice, character, world off balance | slow start, reader unsure what the book is |
| inciting incident | 8-15% | the event that disturbs the status quo | protagonist reacts to nothing in particular |
| first plot point (end of act 1) | 20-25% | protagonist commits; no going back | act 1 feels like setup only |
| pinch point 1 | ~37% | antagonistic force shows its strength | antagonist absent in the middle |
| midpoint | 45-55% | reversal or revelation; reactive to proactive | sagging middle |
| pinch point 2 | ~62% | pressure rises again | stakes plateau |
| second plot point / crisis | 70-80% | all is lost; hardest choice | ending feels cheap |
| climax | 85-95% | central question answered in action | resolution by coincidence or by others |
| resolution | 95-100% | new equilibrium, closing image | long tail or abrupt cut |

## 2. Save the Cat (Blake Snyder, adapted for novels by Jessica Brody)

Opening image (0-1%), theme stated (5%), setup (1-10%), catalyst (10%), debate (10-20%), break into
two (20%), B story (22%), fun and games (20-50%), midpoint (50%), bad guys close in (50-75%), all is
lost (75%), dark night of the soul (75-80%), break into three (80%), finale (80-99%), final image
(99-100%).

Useful questions: is there a debate (does the character resist the call)? Is the B story (often a
relationship) carrying the theme? Does "all is lost" include a whiff of death (literal or
symbolic)? Does the final image mirror the opening image?

## 3. Try-fail cycles

| Outcome | Effect |
|---|---|
| yes, but | progress with a new complication |
| no, and | failure that worsens the situation |
| no | failure; use sparingly (stalls) |
| yes | resolution; mostly at the climax |

A typical arc has at least two escalating failed attempts before success; each attempt should use
what was learned from the last.

## 4. MICE quotient (Orson Scott Card, expanded by Mary Robinette Kowal)

| Thread | Opens when | Closes when |
|---|---|---|
| Milieu | the character enters a place | the character leaves or chooses to stay |
| Inquiry | a question is raised | it is answered |
| Character | a character is dissatisfied with who they are | they change or accept |
| Event | the status quo is disrupted | a new status quo is established |

Threads should close in reverse order of opening (nested like parentheses). A book that opens with
an inquiry should end by answering it.

## 5. Kishotenketsu (four-part, no central conflict)

Introduction, development, twist (an unrelated element), reconciliation. Useful for literary and
quiet fiction where a conflict-driven lens produces false "no stakes" findings.

## 6. Character arc lenses

| Arc | Shape | Audit question |
|---|---|---|
| positive change | believes a lie, confronts truth, changes | where is the lie stated, tested, broken? |
| flat (steadfast) | holds a truth, changes the world | who changes because of the protagonist? |
| negative (fall, corruption, disillusion) | rejects truth or embraces a worse lie | is each step chosen, not imposed? |

Motivation test: for each major choice write "Because she knows X and wants Y, she does Z." If X is
not on the page before Z, it is a motivation gap.
