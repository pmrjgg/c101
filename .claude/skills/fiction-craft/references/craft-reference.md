# Fiction craft reference

## 1. Psychic distance ladder (after John Gardner)

| Level | Example | Use |
|---|---|---|
| 1 far | It was the winter of 1994. A large woman stepped out of a doorway. | openings, transitions, omniscient summaries |
| 2 | Mara Vell had never cared for the marsh in winter. | establishing a character |
| 3 | Mara hated the marsh in winter. | ordinary close third |
| 4 | God, she hated the marsh in winter, the cold that got into the boots. | emotional build |
| 5 near | God, the cold. Boots full of it. Never again. | peaks, panic, revelation |

Jumping from 1 to 5 inside a paragraph jolts the reader; move a step or two at a time unless the jolt
is the point.

## 2. Scene design checklist (expanded)

1. Whose scene is it? (the character with most at stake, or the one who changes most)
2. What do they want on this page, in terms an observer could see?
3. Who or what opposes it, and how does the opposition escalate in two or three steps?
4. What is the turn: the moment the value at stake flips (safe to danger, trust to suspicion)?
5. Outcome type: no / yes-but / no-and.
6. What new question does the reader carry out of the scene?
7. Entry late, exit early: start as close to the turn as orientation allows, leave right after it.

## 3. Dialogue: before and after

Before (on the nose, tagged):
> "I am angry with you because you lied to me about the letter," Mara said angrily.
> "I lied because I was scared you would leave," Tomas explained sadly.

After (subtext, beats):
> "You could have told me." Mara left the envelope on the bench.
> Tomas picked it up, turned it over, set it down further from her. "Tide's turning."

Exposition through conflict instead of "as you know":
> Before: "As you know, our father drowned in the '86 flood when the sluice failed."
> After: "Don't you dare say the sluice to me. You weren't there when they pulled him out."

## 4. Openings that work and why

| Opening move | Why it works | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| character in motion with a want | immediate engagement | action without stakes reads as noise |
| a voice-forward first line | promise of a narrator worth following | cleverness without story |
| a wrong note in an ordinary scene | curiosity | too subtle to notice |
| the end of something (aftermath) | question: what happened? | confusing if over-withheld |

Avoid unless subverted: waking up, weather report, mirror description, dream that turns out to be a
dream, prologue of pure world history.

## 5. Genre notes

- **Thriller.** Chapters 1,500-3,000 words; cliffhanger endings; the antagonist acts off-page and the
  reader feels it; set pieces escalate in scale; the protagonist's plan fails at the midpoint.
- **Mystery.** Body or crime early (first 10-15%); every clue visible to the reader before the
  reveal; red herrings with innocent explanations; the detective's method is consistent.
- **Romance.** Meet in the first chapters; external and internal obstacles; black moment around
  75-85%; grand gesture or earned reconciliation; dual POV common.
- **Fantasy and SF.** Introduce rules through use, not lectures; magic or technology has a cost and
  a limit; the climax is solved with rules the reader already knows (Sanderson's first law as a lens).
- **Literary.** Change may be perception rather than event; sentence-level attention expected;
  ambiguity is allowed but not vagueness about what happens on the page.
- **Horror.** Dread from anticipation, not gore; isolation; normality established first so that its
  violation registers.
- **YA and middle grade.** Voice of the age (not adults imitating teens); agency for the young
  protagonist (adults do not solve the problem); readability targets grade 6-9 (YA) and 4-7 (MG).

## 6. Revision passes (order)

1. Story: scenes that do not turn, missing scenes, order (developmental-editing).
2. Character: motives, arcs, voice distinctness.
3. Scene: entry late, exit early; sequel length.
4. Line: show/tell balance, avoid-prose families, rhythm (prose-style-analysis).
5. Copy: mechanics and consistency (copyediting).
6. Proof: final read of the concatenated manuscript (`manuscript.py concat`).
